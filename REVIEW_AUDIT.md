# Independent submission-readiness audit

**Manuscript:** *Same Words, Different Bets: Lexicons, Static Embeddings and Contextual Pre-training for Sentiment Classification under Scarce Labels and Ambiguity*  
**Materials reviewed:** uploaded 8-page PDF, ACM LaTeX source, full `tiny-nlp-research-v7` source/results archive, saved JSONL/CSV records, prediction arrays, analysis scripts, and slide-deck source/notes.

## Executive verdict

The study has a coherent and potentially useful empirical contribution: it tests a negative result for a very small, laptop-scale MLM run against random initialization, sparse baselines, static embeddings, a lexicon baseline, and public compact checkpoints. The paper is substantially more defensible after correcting the claim/table inconsistencies and separating the SST-5 polarity-strength proxy from actual annotation-related signals.

**It is not yet ready for a full-length submission to a top NLP venue.** The key remaining weakness is experimental evidence, not prose: the learning-rate pilot selects the lowest value in its grid; public checkpoints are not tuned comparably and their predictions are not retained; multi-domain paired significance claims were not reproducibly generated by the archived pipeline; and only three seeds are available. A workshop, student-research track, or carefully scoped short paper is more realistic after the additional experiments below.

## Issues independently confirmed and corrected

1. **Cross-domain direction error.** For random-initialized Tiny BERT, the 4.7-point drop is SST-2 → airline (78.5% to 73.8%), not airline → SST-2. Airline → SST-2 is a 35.1-point drop (91.6% to 56.5%). The finance TF-IDF → airline drop is 60.5 points (87.3% to 26.8%).
2. **Stale conclusion and overgeneralisation.** Removed claims that scale “fixes” the issue, that cross-domain transfer is uniformly poor, and that the deficit disappears on weak-polarity examples. The manuscript now treats the weak-stratum estimate as inconclusive and the public-checkpoint comparison as confounded by vocabulary, corpus, and training recipe.
3. **VADER is not a consumer of the unlabeled corpus.** The paper and slides now describe VADER as an external, zero-task-label lexicon baseline, distinct from static embeddings and MLM trained on the movie-review corpus. The ACM label-equivalence table includes the VADER row.
4. **Fairness language.** Replaced “identical tokenised inputs/head/optimiser for every model” with the narrower, accurate statement: splits, label budgets, test sets, and perturbation procedures are shared where applicable; tokenizers, feature representations, and optimization settings differ by model family.
5. **Bootstrap scope.** Clarified that the existing SST-2 paired bootstrap resamples test sentences after averaging per-sentence correctness differences across matched seeds. It quantifies test-example uncertainty conditional on those seeds; it does **not** capture retraining variability.
6. **OOD uncertainty.** Clarified that approximately 2.4 percentage points is a binomial standard error near 65% accuracy on 400 reviews, not a 95% confidence-interval margin. The manuscript no longer calls the smallest OOD gaps significant without paired intervals.
7. **Ambiguity interpretation.** The SST-5 strong/weak split is described as a polarity-intensity proxy, not annotator disagreement. Weak-stratum non-significance is not treated as evidence that an effect disappears or that the sentences are genuinely ambiguous.
8. **Probe trajectory wording.** Changed “rises steadily” to “increases overall, with an intermediate dip,” and toned down the claim that the frozen representation is generally better.
9. **Cross-domain statistics and metrics.** The generated transfer table now includes accuracy and Macro-F1, mean and standard deviation over three neural seeds, and deterministic TF-IDF results. Finance/Airline in-domain learning curves now have a reproducible Macro-F1 table alongside accuracy. A compact full-budget ambiguity table now reports Financial PhraseBank agreement tiers and Twitter Airline crowd-worker confidence separately.
10. **Unsupported multi-domain significance claims.** Removed the claim that finance/airline models are statistically indistinguishable: the archived scripts do not reproduce the corresponding paired full-data intervals. Also removed the previously quoted airline n=100 confidence intervals from the main narrative pending reproducible regeneration.
11. **Finance results reconciled with the table.** The paper now notes that MLM is slightly higher at finance n=50 (69.5% vs 68.9%) but lower from n=100 onward, rather than claiming it loses at every budget.
12. **Reproducibility workflow.** `run_all.sh` now runs all three domains by default, supports `--core-only`, reuses an existing MLM checkpoint under `--resume`, and fails safely if old result logs exist but their matching MLM checkpoint is missing. The deck builder no longer uses a hard-coded `/home/claude/...` path.
13. **Submission formatting.** The ACM source is now an anonymous review draft rather than containing fake institution, email, and venue placeholders. Venue-specific metadata must be added once the target venue is selected.
14. **Slides and speaker notes.** Updated to match the corrected cross-domain directions, qualify ambiguity claims, distinguish VADER from corpus-trained methods, and remove unsupported multi-domain significance claims.

## Remaining blockers before submission

### Must do

1. **Expand the learning-rate search.** The current pilot searched five values from `2e-4` to `4e-3` and selected the lower boundary (`2e-4`) for both initializations. Add values below `2e-4` and give random-init and MLM models the same tuning budget. Then rerun the affected experiments and regenerate all results. Until then, phrase the result as “this tested recipe did not help,” not “small-scale MLM does not help.”
2. **Run the new multi-domain paired analysis.** After datasets are accessible, run `python src/cross_domain_stats.py --bootstrap 2000` (or `cd src && python cross_domain_stats.py --bootstrap 2000`) and replace descriptive comparisons with intervals only where the generated results support them. The intervals remain conditional on the saved seeds.
3. **Use Macro-F1 consistently for imbalanced domains.** Finance and airline are imbalanced, but early stopping currently selects by dev accuracy. Either make Macro-F1 the selection metric for those domains and rerun, or justify accuracy selection and report Macro-F1 as a co-primary/secondary metric explicitly.
4. **Tune public checkpoints and save predictions.** BERT-Tiny and DistilBERT use fixed, untuned learning rates, and their prediction arrays are absent. Tune them under a declared budget and save per-example predictions if making inferential comparisons; otherwise keep their role as exploratory reference points.
5. **Add independent seeds.** Three seeds are useful but weak evidence for broad generalization claims. Aim for at least five seeds for the central random-init vs MLM comparisons if compute permits.
6. **Freeze venue-specific claims and formatting.** Once a target is chosen, check its page limit, anonymous-review rules, citation style, supplementary-material policy, and artifact requirements. Do not use the generic ACM draft unchanged for an ACL/EMNLP submission.

### Strongly recommended

- Keep the main claim narrow: the tested 1.2M-word, 5,000-step, 13-minute CPU recipe did not improve downstream SST-2 performance over random initialization and strong sparse baselines.
- Do not claim a new method. The contribution is a controlled empirical comparison, a carefully qualified negative result, and analysis of label budget, robustness, efficiency, and domain-specific ambiguity signals.
- State dataset licensing/provenance explicitly, especially the Financial PhraseBank non-commercial/share-alike terms and the less-clear redistribution status of the Twitter Airline dataset.
- Report the target-domain majority-class baseline alongside cross-domain accuracy, and retain Macro-F1 because finance and airline have opposite class skews.
- Do not equate Financial PhraseBank agreement tiers with Twitter crowd-worker confidence: they are related but distinct operationalizations.

## Reproduction commands

From the project root, on a machine with the required Python packages and dataset-download access:

```bash
bash run_all.sh                  # clean, three-domain base study
bash run_all.sh --resume         # resume without overwriting an existing MLM checkpoint
python -m py_compile src/*.py tests/*.py
pytest -q tests
```

The full three-domain run is CPU-intensive and may take hours. Public Hugging Face checkpoint benchmarking remains optional and requires model downloads. After the experiments finish, regenerate the paired multi-domain intervals before restoring any significance claim in the manuscript.
